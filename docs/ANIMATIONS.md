# Animation pipeline

The full list of the 53 clips (with durations and which grip-set variants exist) is in [ASSETS.md](ASSETS.md); it is generated from the shipped GLB.

## Third-person (`src/character/soldier.ts`)
- **Clips come from the file, not assumptions.** Names are looked up at runtime; a missing grip-set variant falls back to the base clip. Gaps that matter: there is **no `fire_sidearm`, `reload_sidearm`, `jump_sidearm`, `aim_sidearm`, or any `*_blade` for fire/reload/jump**, so pistols and the knife use the base `fire` / `reload` / `jump`, and melee uses `melee`.
- **Upper/lower split.** Every full-body clip is split once (by bone name) into an upper-body half (`Spine, Chest, Neck, Head, Shoulder/UpperArm/LowerArm/Hand L+R`) and a lower-body half. Locomotion (`idle / walk / run / crouch / jump / work`) plays on both; **fire, reload, hit and melee play on the upper half only** with a fast blend, so the legs keep running while the arms shoot or reload. Weights are managed manually and cross-faded (≈70 ms).
- **Sync with game state.** Actions are triggered from the simulation's timestamps (`firedAt`, `reloadAt`, `hurtAt`, `diedAt`), never from the animation, so the clip cannot contradict the game: a reload clip only plays if the sim started a reload (`reloadT > 0`), death plays `die` once and holds, a respawn resets the mixer. Reload playback is time-scaled to the weapon's reload duration (capped at 2.6 s).
- **Aim pitch** is applied procedurally to `Spine` (40 %) and `Chest` (60 %) after the mixer runs.
- **Play-speed matching:** run/walk time-scale follows the real horizontal speed to limit foot sliding (reference speeds 4.4 and 1.9 m/s were tuned by eye).
- **Culling:** off-screen soldiers beyond 8 m keep their pose (no mixer update); death poses are kept.

## Weapons on the soldier
Evidence: `docs/evidence/weapon-hold-before-after.jpg` (real captures from the QA viewer, same poses before and after).

**What was wrong (found by measuring, not by eye).** The first version attached each gun with a yaw about the socket's Y axis and offsets "set by eye". Probing the world matrices showed the hand sockets are authored with **+X forward, +Y toward the wearer's right, +Z down**, so that transform put the gun's *up* on the socket's +Y: every gun was **rolled 90°** (sights pointing sideways), the idle barrel pointed ~45° off the soldier's facing in the fire pose, and the left hand floated ~10 cm under a bare barrel.

**What it does now**
- **Orientation:** model +Z (muzzle) → socket +X, model +Y (up) → socket −Z, model +X (gun-left) → socket −Y (`WEAPON_TO_SOCKET` in `soldier.ts`). Verified numerically: gun roll ≤ 13° in every pose (the residue is the animators' own wrist angle), barrel yaw 0.0°.
- **Hold points** (`src/weapons/holds.ts`, one table shared with the first-person viewmodel): the pistol-grip centre goes on `Socket_Hand_R`; a support-hand point under the forend/handguard. Every number was read off the baked geometry's side profile (`tools/scratch/weapon-profile.ts`), e.g. the A5 grip is at z = −0.20 m, not at the model's centre.
- **Handguard:** the CC0 rifles/SMG/marksman rifle have only a thin bare barrel where the support hand should be, so a small procedural faceted handguard + top rail (`guardParts`, ~100 triangles, merged into the same single draw call) is added; the shotgun already has a forend. This is the only change to the kit weapons' look and it is deliberate.
- **Support-hand IK:** `src/character/ik.ts` is an analytic two-bone solve (UpperArm_L → LowerArm_L → Hand_L, elbow keeps the side the animation chose) run **after** the mixer and the aim pitch. It puts `Socket_Hand_L` on the hold point, then orients the palm to the animators' own left-in-right-hand orientation (measured on the kit's rifle clips, < 4° spread). The wrist socket sits 4 cm from the wrist bone, so reach/orient run twice. Measured residual: **0.0 cm** on every rifle/SMG/shotgun/marksman pose.
- **IK weight** is 1 for rifle-set weapons and fades out when the clip itself moves the hand: reload (hand goes to the magazine and returns at both ends), half weight during a hit flinch, 0 while planting/defusing, dead, or holding a pistol/knife (those kit clips are one-handed).
- **Torso untwist:** the upper-body overlays (fire/reload/…) are cut from clips whose *hips* were twisted toward the aim; without those hips the torso and gun ended up up to 45° off the facing direction. The barrel's real direction is measured after the mixer and the spine/chest are untwisted about the vertical axis so it points where the player looks (not for the knife, and not while working). Idle keeps the animators' lowered "low-ready" pitch.
- Muzzle flash is a pooled additive sprite on the barrel axis just past the muzzle (cross + a side-on streak so it reads from the side); tracers start from it.
- The pack's built-in `open`/`close` clips are **not** reload animations and are not used. Each world weapon is baked into a single vertex-coloured mesh at runtime (1 draw call).
- **QA:** `node tools/holdtest.cjs` (needs `npm run dev`) checks 8 weapon/team variants × 5 poses: roll, barrel yaw, support-hand-to-forend distance. 40/40 pass.
- Known limits: the pistol clips hold the gun low (about −45° idle, −69° crouched) because that is how the kit animates sidearms; pistols and the knife are one-handed in third person; the left arm in the middle of a reload is the kit clip's, not IK.

## The pack's weapons float apart — and how that is fixed (`src/weapons/fillers.ts`)
Evidence: `docs/evidence/weapon-parts-before-after.jpg`.

The CC0 Hill Town weapons are kit-bashed from separate chunky pieces **and ship without a lower receiver**: the pistol grip, trigger guard and magazine hang 3–6 cm below the upper receiver, the front sight post floats 2.5 cm above the barrel, the SMG has no rear receiver at all, the marksman rifle's scope floats 1.4 cm over its rail, and the knife's pommel rings sit behind the end of the handle core. I measured this on the untouched original download as well as the shipped GLB (`tools/weapon-connectivity.ts`: triangles are welded into pieces, pieces are joined when their closest vertex-to-triangle distance is < 5 mm), so it is how the asset is, not something the optimiser broke. In play it looked like parts detached from the gun.

Fix: a small table of filler blocks per weapon (pistol-grip neck, lower receiver / magwell, front-sight base, scope mounts, SMG rear receiver, knife handle core), each ~3 mm overlapping its neighbours and coloured from the neighbouring kit material. Both the first-person model and the merged third-person model use the same table. `tests/weapons.test.ts` first proves the shipped GLB really is fragmented (so the fix is needed), then proves every weapon is **one connected object** with the blocks in, and that the hand grip points sit on solid geometry. The pistol was already one piece.

## First-person viewmodel — PROVISIONAL (`src/weapons/viewmodel.ts`)
The CuiGames A5 / P50 / T12 animated viewmodels were **not obtained** (see ASSETS.md). Until they are, the first-person weapon is the CC0 world model rendered close-up in its own pass (cleared depth buffer, so it never clips into walls).
Evidence: `docs/evidence/viewmodel-before-after.jpg` (QA viewer, same poses before/after) and `docs/evidence/ingame-first-person-hands.jpg` (the real game, rifle + pistol, idle + firing, software GL).

**Hands.** The first version drew each arm as a box with a block on the end and positioned the hands by hand-typed numbers; seen from the side the gun floated above the hands. Now both gloved hands are built from capsules + rounded boxes (palm/back-of-hand, four fingers wrapped round the grip or forend, thumb) and are **placed from the same hold table as the third-person soldier**: the firing hand's palm origin is the model's grip point, the support hand's is the forend point, carried through the animated rig every frame, so hands stay on the gun through recoil, bob, sway, inspect, equip and the reload tilt. Sleeved forearms run from each wrist back to an off-screen elbow. Pistols use a two-handed grip; the knife is a fist round the handle.
**Animation (procedural, driven by the sim):** idle breathing, walk bob and look sway (off with *Reduce motion*), recoil kick scaled per weapon, pistol slide cycling back each shot, shotgun pump racking after each shot, equip raise, **staged reload** (gun lifts and cants toward the player, support hand goes to the magazine's real bounds, the magazine drops away, a fresh one slides in, slap/charge), inspect, knife swipe, ejected brass from the ejection side, muzzle flash on the barrel axis.
**QA:** `?viewer=viewmodel&weapon=a5&state=idle|fire|reload&k=0.4&cam=x,y,z,tx,ty,tz` renders the player's exact view or a debug camera; `node tools/vmtest.cjs` checks all weapons idle + firing: muzzle and hands on screen, barrel within 12° of straight ahead, gun upright, hands within 1 mm of their hold points (14/14).
Honest limits: the hands are low-poly procedural gloves, not a sculpted model; the stock of long guns is a large dark slab near the camera (it is the real model's buttstock at close range); reload poses are approximate and were checked in stills, not in motion.
When real GLB viewmodels are imported, the intended mapping per weapon profile is: `Equip`→equip, `Idle`→idle, `Fire_Auto_Start/Loop/End` (or `Recoil_Stylized`)→fire, `Reload_Showcase`→reload (time-scaled to `WeaponDef.reloadTime`), `Inspect`→inspect. This mapping is **not implemented or tested**, because it needs the real files.
